# Call For Articles

### An Anthology of Sinophone Media Theory

## Introduction

We are envisioning a collective project to collect and translate the most original ideas from Sinophone media theory in the PRC.

This project draws inspiration from a series of new efforts to introduce media theories from non-Western origins to an English-speaking audience to fill a major gap. On the one hand, Chinese media theory in the mainstream university model of knowledge is heavily guided by translations of Euro-American theories; on the other, the original Chinese-language media-theoretical discourse that emerged among para-academic, artistic, and grassroots circles is almost entirely invisible in the Euro-American theoretical map. This project aims at locating and curating an alternative interdisciplinary intellectual history of media theory (electronic, computational, and internet media) in the PRC, in various formats of writings from academic, para-academic, artistic and independent publishing to para-academic and technical discussions, to colloquial “low” theories circulated on the internet.

## Why are we doing this

First, this project is not simply foregrounding Chinese-language media theory for the sake of diversifying European media theory. Second, the objective of this proposed research is not “de-Westernising” Chinese scholarship; that is, this project opts out of locating supporting facts for a radical alterity isolated in China’s own intellectual history (i.e. in classical thoughts). A complete “decolonialization” of knowledge—a severance or decoupling—is unlikely to happen in the current epistemological disposition nor should it be a desirable objective in a realistic sense. Methodological nationalism (an Asian or Chinese axis) cannot be an end in itself. Otherwise, it is tautological and as essentialising as Eurocentrism.

The field of media theory has been long dominated by theorists of Euro-American origin but for those of us in Asia, this assessment comes with a sense of deadlock: the response is either a reactionary methodological nationalism—“we can be better at this game of Europeans than the Europeans themselves”/ “the Chinese situation is unique and therefore we need to reinvent the wheel”—or a complacent acceptance of this hierarchy of knowledge production in the dominant academic recipe of “a local case study channelled through a analytical toolset provided by an European theory” or “modifying and adapting existing theories to suit the [Asian] context” (Ma 2000: 32). We aim to break from this cycle, in order to look for a new structure of theory. This is not a philosophical project as it is not preoccupied with the theme of essentialisation at all (whether for or against), nor highly specialized knowledge that can only be secured by creating sub-sub-disciplines in perpetuity.

This proposed project will be the first project that aims at collecting, curating, translating and annotating an intellectual history in the sphere of Chinese-language media theory and computational culture by collecting both primary sources—this is especially in short supply since the vast majority of primary sources are seen as “raw materials”, to be processed/fed into the refinery known as Western “theory”, rather than being recognized as worthy (attempts at) “theories” in themselves—and scholarly/para-academic works by artist-researchers and “grassroots (minjian) intellectuals” (Veg 2019).

The project is not interested in answering the question whether the system of socio-technical knowledge in China is universal (European) or relational/local (Chinese), but to acknowledge that in practice epistemological systems do operate sometimes in parallel, sometimes in exchange, sometimes even antagonistically or paradoxically—we need to pause the drive to ontologise and critique until we have a better picture of the existing “low theory” (Harney and Moten 2013) of public knowledge that is being circulated among the masses. This “low-ness” first and foremost set itself against the sweeping discourse about media, technics, culture, nation and so on. This low-ness refers to the unofficial, un-institutionalized, independent (self-funded or semi-professional) intellectual workers. The project is concerned with format, platform, infrastructure, hardware, software, intellectual communities, language, and the morphology of media ecology over time in China. Second, this low theory is deliberately not to dumb down nor is it a blank slate of interdisciplinarity—it is a slice through all these, “a moving image that would zigzag its way across all these heterogenous types of knowledge” (Mackay 2020). Third, there is a certain materiality in this low-ness of knowledge, whether it is referring to the materiality of computational hardware, network infrastructure or tactile physicality of print publications.

## Scope of the Collection

In addition to the obviously topical scholarship in academic journals, we would like to focus on:

- books,
- monographs,
- edited volumes,
- magazines,
- zines,
- personal websites or blogs (WeChat publications),
- mailing lists,
- artist collectives,
- and other multimodal networks and activities.

Historical scope (referring to publication dates of the suggested texts): mostly in the past three decades, but it can be also earlier.

Themes, including but not limited to:

- Software (criticism, code, open source…)
- Hardware (necrohardware, learning computer, history of computation…)
- Platforms (WeChat, pirate radio…)
- Infrastructure
- Internet culture/history/aesthetics
- Literature and digital writing (web lit, electronic lit, etc)
- Games
- Cybernetics

It must be emphasised that the project does not only focus on individuals (i.e. individual scholars or schools or academic “stars”). We do want these authors; but if the particular scholarship is already well-known outside of China, we would like to avoid. This is not just a project in the style of humanities intellectual history but also a curatorial and archival project.
